Vector Algebra and Component Addition

A whiteboard lecture: vectors as geometry first, then components, trigonometry, and the algebra of putting several vectors back together.

Lecture
01

Setup

This lesson is completely lecture-based. I work through vectors on the whiteboard, drawing each one as I go. The slides give us the geometric and algebraic pictures, but I build the mathematics on the board with the students rather than presenting finished solutions.

02

What Happens

I begin with addition and subtraction as geometry, drawing vectors tip-to-tail. Then we resolve vectors into \(x\) and \(y\) components, connect the diagram to the trigonometry, and work through the algebra. We finish by adding several vectors component by component and recombining the result into a magnitude and direction.

03

Field Notes

Some students struggle more with the geometry or algebra than with the physics itself. I have taught vectors outside with rulers and meter sticks, having students physically construct them, but for this lesson I prefer the traditional whiteboard. Vectors matter in IB Physics, but vector algebra is not a major standalone topic. Over time I have become more comfortable not reteaching every piece of mathematics in the physics classroom. The mathematics courses can teach the structure. Part of the student's job is learning how to transfer it into physics, and this lesson helps with that transition.

Equipment

  • Whiteboard and markers
  • Vector slides

Physics

Geometry: add vectors head-to-tail; subtract by adding the opposite vector.

Components: \(a_x = a\cos\theta\), \(a_y = a\sin\theta\).

Resultant: \(R_x = \sum a_x\), \(R_y = \sum a_y\), \(R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2}\), with direction from \(\theta = \arctan(R_y / R_x)\) and the correct quadrant.
